Privacy Policy

Life Game Project · lifegameproject.com · last updated 26 October 2026

This policy covers Life Game Project's website and the iPhone and iPad apps named below, including Qi. We try to collect as little as possible. The first sections describe the Game of Life website; the app and online-room sections explain what those games store and what leaves your device.

What stays on your device

  • Your language choice, saved patterns/blueprints, lesson progress, and game settings are stored locally in your browser (localStorage). They never leave your device and we cannot see them.
  • The app works fully offline.

What you choose to submit

  • Leaderboard: if you submit a challenge score, the display name you type and your score are sent to and stored on our leaderboard so they can be shown publicly. Please don't use your real name if you'd rather stay anonymous — any nickname works.
  • Community gallery: if you share a pattern, that pattern and the name you attach to it are stored and shown publicly in the gallery.

Submitting to the leaderboard or gallery is always optional — the game is fully playable without it.

What we do NOT collect

  • No accounts, no sign-in, no passwords.
  • No advertising, no third-party ad/tracking SDKs, no location, no contacts.
  • No analytics that identify you personally.

iPhone and iPad apps

The native apps Manilha, Game Life, Qi and Plank Constant have no accounts, leaderboards, galleries, analytics or advertising. Your language choice, game settings and bests are stored only on your device and are deleted when you delete the app. Game Life and Plank Constant send nothing to us. Manilha and Qi send online-room data when you choose to play online with friends. Qi also requests the online server address at startup. The following sections explain these requests and Plank Constant's use of the camera.

Manilha: online rooms

You can play Manilha with up to three friends in an online room, from the Manilha app on iPhone or iPad or in a browser at lifegameproject.com/game/truco.html. Solo and same-device games never go online.

  • What is sent: when you create or join a room, your device connects to our room server (the "relay", which runs on Cloudflare) and sends the room code; a random player number your device makes up for that room to associate your moves with your seat; the animal avatar and seat you pick; and your moves (how many tricks you guess and which cards you play). The player who hosts the table also sends the state of the game: everyone's cards and scores.
  • What is not sent: no name, account, email, chat, contacts, location or advertising identifier. Nobody types anything that other players see: everyone appears as an animal and a seat number.
  • Who can see it: everyone in the room receives the room's data so their device can show the game. That includes the other players' cards, which their apps and the web page keep hidden but a technically minded player could read, so rooms are meant for people you trust.
  • How long it is kept: the relay stores room data to run your game and automatically deletes it after about six hours without a game-data update. Connection keep-alives do not extend this period. We do not use room data for advertising, tracking or analytics.
  • Cloudflare: Cloudflare operates the relay and handles connection data, including your IP address, to deliver the service, under its privacy policy.

Qi: online rooms

You can play Go, Gomoku or Chess with a friend, or Mahjong with up to four players, in an online room from the Qi app on iPhone or iPad or in a browser at lifegameproject.com/game/qi.html. Games against the computer and games shared on one device do not use the online relay.

  • Server configuration: at startup, the app makes an HTTPS request to lifegameproject.com/game/truco-relay.json for the current relay address. This request does not include a player ID, room code or gameplay content. The website host handles ordinary connection data, such as your IP address, to serve the request.
  • What is sent: when you create or join a room, your device connects to the same room server as Manilha (the "relay", which runs on Cloudflare). It sends the room code; a random player ID generated for that room; your seat; the game and board settings; and gameplay content, including moves, take-back and draw requests, Go dead-stone marks, Mahjong discards and claims, scores and rematch requests. The host sends the shared game record. Mahjong also sends public encryption keys, encrypted tile data and encrypted host notes.
  • Player IDs and purpose: a room's random ID associates your moves with your seat and lets you return to that seat. It is not your name, an account ID or an advertising ID. The relay stores the ID with the room's gameplay content only to operate and synchronise online play. We do not use it for advertising, tracking or analytics.
  • What is not sent: no name, account, email, chat, contacts, location or advertising identifier.
  • Who can see it: devices connected to the room receive its data to show the game. Anyone who has the room code can access the room while it exists, so share it only with people you intend to play with. Public moves and scores are visible to the room.
  • Mahjong hands: the host's device deals and knows every tile. Tiles sent privately to a guest are encrypted for that guest using X25519 key agreement, HKDF-SHA256 and ChaCha20-Poly1305; the iPhone and iPad app uses Apple's CryptoKit. Other guests and the relay receive encrypted tile data rather than that guest's readable hand. At the end of a hand, the host publishes the information needed for everyone to reveal and replay it. These protections do not hide tiles from the host.
  • How long it is kept: the relay automatically deletes room data, including player IDs and gameplay content, after about six hours without a game-data update. Connection keep-alives do not extend this period. Leaving a room or forgetting it on your device does not immediately delete its relay data.
  • What stays on your device: Qi remembers the last room's code, your random player ID and, for Mahjong, your room secret so you can return to the game. The × next to the saved room on the start screen removes that saved room information from the app.
  • Cloudflare: Cloudflare operates the relay and handles connection data, including your IP address, to deliver the service, under its privacy policy.

Plank Constant: camera and face data

Plank Constant uses the front TrueDepth camera, through Apple's ARKit, to follow your face so you can play hands-free while you hold a plank.

  • What it reads: while you set up and play, ARKit tracks your face and gives the app a few measurements: where your face is in the picture, how far it is from the phone, which way your head is tilted or turned, and how raised your eyebrows are. The app uses them only to steer, strike and block, and to tell you when you are too close or too far.
  • Pixel selfie: the app makes a tiny 20 × 20-pixel picture of your face to use as your character's head. It is kept in memory for the current round only and is never saved.
  • Nothing is stored or sent to us: no photos, video, face measurements or face maps are saved on your device or uploaded. Face data is never used for advertising or marketing, never used to identify you, and never sold or shared with anyone.
  • Duels with a friend over FaceTime (SharePlay): if you start a duel with a friend over SharePlay, your phone sends theirs your moves (strike, block or rest), whether your face is in view, and your 20 × 20-pixel selfie, so it can appear on your character. This goes between your two phones through Apple's SharePlay service; we never receive it, and your friend's phone does not save it.
  • Your choice: you can play with touch instead of the camera, and you can turn camera access off at any time in Settings → Plank Constant.
